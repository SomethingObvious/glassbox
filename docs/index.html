Inside LLM Inference, Drawn From Real Runs of Small Public Models

Paul Wu, github.com/SomethingObvious

Abstract

A one-layer EAGLE-3 head reads Qwen3-1.7B's own hidden states and guesses the next four tokens, and Qwen3-1.7B checks all four in one forward pass. Guesses up to the first miss are kept, and the big model adds one token of its own. With greedy decoding the text is exactly what the big model writes alone, just sooner. Every day Figure 1 sums up the top Hacker News story that way on GitHub's CPUs and races it against the big model writing alone, and the button below runs one on your GPU with two guesses a pass.

Mixture-of-Experts Routing

IBM Granite 3.0 1B-A400M has 32 experts in each of its 24 layers, and a small router sends every token to 8 of them. Figure 2 shows the router's real decisions from one forward pass over each sentence. Pick a sentence, then hover or tap its tokens.

Rounding Weights to Fewer Bits

Qwen3-0.6B ships its weights in bf16, and every token it writes reads all of them from memory. Storing them in 8 or 4 bits makes each token cheaper, and the price is rounding. Figure 3 snaps one real row of weights to a grid, then shows what each format does to the text the whole model writes.

A group of weights shares one grid, stretched from its smallest weight to its largest. One outlier stretches the grid for its whole group, so the rest fall onto a few levels near zero. Smaller groups keep that damage local, at the cost of more scales to store.

Watching the Answer Form

A model only commits to a token at its last layer, but every layer's output can be read the same way, through the final norm and the output head. Figure 4 does that for each of Qwen3-0.6B's 28 layers at every position of a prompt. This is the logit lens. Hover or tap a cell to see its five likeliest tokens.

For the French prompt, the layers just before the end guess 犬, the Chinese and Japanese word for dog, before the last layer settles on "ch" for chien. Studies of other multilingual models have seen the same thing, with the middle layers working in the language they saw most in training.
